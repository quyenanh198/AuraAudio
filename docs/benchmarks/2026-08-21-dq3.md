## CPU timing (real, this machine)

- 30s clip: 18.90s separation compute time
- 60s clip: 27.88s separation compute time
- Linear fit from these two points: `elapsed_s ≈ 9.9 + 0.30 * clip_duration_s`
  (sub-linear -- a fixed per-call overhead of ~10s dominates short clips,
  marginal cost is only ~0.3x real-time once warmed up). Extrapolated to a
  5-minute (300s) song: **≈ 100s (≈1.7 minutes) of extra separation
  compute** -- well under the 10-minutes-extra threshold this item's hard
  constraints flag as a UX concern. Model load is a one-time cost per
  worker process (lazy singleton, `aura_worker.separation._load_model`),
  not paid per job after the first.
- Job progress: the `separate` stage is its own pipeline stage
  (`STAGE_PROGRESS["separate"] = 18`, between probe's 10 and normalize's
  25), so the Home screen's existing per-stage progress label shows
  "separate" while this runs, giving the user a real reason for the extra
  wait rather than a silent stall.

| open-unmix (`umxhq`) | Not evaluated further | Demucs cleared every hard constraint (pins, weights, CPU time) and its own `htdemucs_6s` variant already targets this app's exact instrument set (guitar/piano stems) — no evidence-based reason surfaced to evaluate a second candidate in the time budget available. |

**Model selection within Demucs**: the default 4-stem `htdemucs` (84MB) was

`htdemucs_6s` (55MB, smaller) already offers everything needed once the
stem-mapping decision below settled on summing two of its stems.

## Stem-mapping decision

This is the part of this item that most benefited from measuring instead
of assuming. The investigation went through two rounds:

**Round 1 (looked clean, was wrong): target the "other" stem alone.**
`htdemucs_6s` was tested on a synthetic guitar-melody-plus-vocal-and-
percussion mix: `other`-alone onset F1 was **1.000** (vs 0.800 for the raw
unseparated mix, vs 0.200 for the model's OWN dedicated `guitar` stem on
that same fixture). This looked like a clean, unambiguous win and was the
first thing committed to `aura_worker.separation`.

**Round 2 (real benchmark run surfaced the flaw): a second, contradictory
fixture.** Running the real, committed `dq3_mixed_benchmark.py` script
against a SECOND mixed fixture -- a guitar arpeggio over a sustained pad
chord, no drums/vocals-like content at all -- produced **0 detected notes**
with `other`-alone separation (a `NO_MUSIC_DETECTED` failure, not just a
quality regression). The SAME script's clean-guitar-suite pass (separation
ON, no interference at all) also regressed 3 of 7 fixtures, one by -0.381
F1. Diagnosed directly (not guessed): a per-stem energy probe on the pad
fixture showed `other` stem RMS 0.052 (indistinguishable from the ~0.05
noise floor shared by `drums`/`bass`/`vocals`/`piano` on that fixture) while
the model's own `guitar` stem carried RMS 0.170 -- **the model had put
almost the entire real guitar signal into its OWN `guitar` stem this
time**, the opposite of what happened with vocal+percussion interference
present. A fixed single-stem target is fundamentally the wrong strategy
for this model: which stem carries the real signal depends on what else is
in the mix.

**Fix: sum the `guitar` and `other` stems.** Re-running the identical
before/after comparison with `aura_worker.separation.separate_guitar`
summing both stems: the pad fixture recovered to F1 0.700 (delta -0.062,
essentially the OFF baseline, not a total failure), the vocal+percussion
fixture's win was fully preserved (F1 1.000, delta +0.200 unchanged), and
the clean-suite regressions shrank from a worst case of -0.381 to a worst
case of -0.176 (4 of 7 fixtures now unchanged or improved). This is the
shipped mapping -- see `aura_worker.separation`'s module docstring for the
same evidence trail inline with the code.

**Piano was never viable with this stem mapping.** A real-piano-sample
mixed fixture (piano chords + vocal + percussion, per this item's own
"real-piano samples layered with synthesized vocals" guidance) showed the
piano-instrument code path (dedicated `piano` stem, tested in isolation
during investigation, not the shipped `guitar`+`other` mapping which is
guitar-specific) landing BELOW the unseparated baseline on average across
repeated runs -- consistent with `htdemucs_6s`'s own upstream
documentation explicitly warning "the piano source doesn't work so well at
the moment". `aura_worker.separation` and `aura_worker.stages.separate`
are therefore guitar-only: a piano project with the setting enabled is a
documented no-op (verified directly in this report's own mixed-fixture
row above -- `piano_chords_mixed_vocal_percussion`'s OFF and ON columns
are IDENTICAL, 0.3125 both times, confirming the gate works exactly as
designed, not accidentally skipped).

## Ship decision

**Recommendation: ship, opt-in, guitar only.** All hard constraints are
met with one disclosed, non-blocking risk:

- **Pins**: clean on Linux/Windows/macOS-arm64; one disclosed, narrow,
  non-build-verified caveat on macOS Intel (see "Resolution check" above).
- **Weights license**: MIT code; the pretrained weights carry a real,
  disclosed provenance risk (MUSDB18-HQ's non-commercial training-data
  license) that a human reviewer with legal context should independently
  weigh in on -- see "License record" above. Not treated as a hard blocker
  given these exact weights' widespread existing commercial use, but this
  is the one part of this item's evidence that is a judgment call, not a
  clean pass.
- **CPU time**: ~1.7 minutes of extra compute for a 5-minute song,
  comfortably under the 10-minutes-extra threshold. A `separate` job
  stage makes the wait visible, not silent.
- **Benchmark**: a real, reproducible win on the interference pattern this
  item exists for (vocals + percussion, the shape of a real YouTube-import
  mix), a small and disclosed cost on both a harder interference pattern
  (a sustained pad, F1 -0.062) and on clean solo audio (worst case -0.176,
  most fixtures unchanged) -- honest tradeoffs of an OPT-IN feature a user
  chooses specifically because their recording has more than just the
  target instrument in it, not a feature that changes anything for anyone
  who leaves it unchecked.

If a stricter reading of the weights-license risk is wanted: the revert is
small and localized -- the `separate` stage's call site in
`aura_worker.runner.run_transcription_job` and
`aura_worker.eval.pipeline.run_pipeline_stages` is a single `if` gate;
removing it (and leaving `aura_worker.separation`/`aura_worker.stages.
separate`/the fetch script/the frontend checkbox in place, inert) fully
disables the feature without touching anything else.
