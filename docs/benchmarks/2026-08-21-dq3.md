## Determinism (fixed after code review; read this before the tables below)

**Finding**: an independent from-scratch review run did not reproduce 3 of
the 10 non-piano deltas this document originally reported (the exact
"worst case" numbers the Ship decision leaned on). The piano no-op rows
reproduced bit-identically across runs, which pointed the diagnosis
straight at the demucs call itself rather than anything in the surrounding
pipeline (normalize/inference/scoring are all otherwise deterministic).

**Root cause, found by direct measurement, not upstream's docs**: `demucs.
apply.apply_model`'s own `shifts` parameter defaults to `1`, not `0`. This
is not floating-point non-associativity noise — it is a genuinely RANDOM
test-time-augmentation trick (shift the input by a random offset, run the
model, shift back). Isolated proof: two back-to-back calls on the
IDENTICAL decoded input tensor, in the same process, produced raw stem
output differing by up to 14% of the stem's own peak amplitude — for
reference, plain CPU floating-point reduction-order noise would be many
orders of magnitude smaller than that. Thread-pinning
(`torch.set_num_threads(1)`) alone, tried first as the obvious suspect,
did NOT fix it — confirming the random shift, not parallel scheduling, was
the actual cause.

**Fix**: `aura_worker.separation.separate_guitar` now passes `shifts=0`
explicitly to `apply_model`, disabling the augmentation. Verified directly
(isolated test: `torch.equal` on the raw output tensor) that this gives
bit-for-bit identical results both IN-PROCESS and ACROSS SEPARATE PROCESS
INVOCATIONS, at the DEFAULT thread count — no thread-pinning needed, and
it did not slow anything down (if anything, removing the extra
shift-and-average pass made separation faster — see "CPU timing" below,
now lower than this document's first version reported). A regression test
(`test_separate_guitar_is_deterministic_across_calls` in
`workers/transcription/tests/test_separation.py`) pins two independent
calls producing byte-identical WAV output.

**Re-verification protocol**: `workers/transcription/scripts/
dq3_mixed_benchmark.py` was run **4 separate times** (3 back-to-back for
this fix, plus the run that produced this document's own numbers) after
the fix landed. Every one of the 10 mixed/clean onset-F1 deltas was
**bit-identical across all 4 runs** (compared at full float precision, not
rounded) — full determinism, not just a tightened variance band, so no
mean±range reporting was needed. The tables below are that stable,
reproduced result — not the pre-fix numbers.

## CPU timing (real, this machine, averaged over 4 runs)

- 30s clip: mean 12.6s (range 11.2–13.5s across the 4 runs) separation compute time
- 60s clip: mean 21.3s (range 20.9–22.2s across the 4 runs) separation compute time
- Linear fit through the two means: `elapsed_s ≈ 3.9 + 0.29 * clip_duration_s`
  (sub-linear — a fixed per-call overhead dominates short clips).
  Extrapolated to a 5-minute (300s) song: **≈ 91s (≈1.5 minutes) of extra
  separation compute** — under the pre-fix estimate too (removing the
  random-shift augmentation's extra forward pass made this faster, not
  slower) and comfortably under the 10-minutes-extra UX threshold this
  item's hard constraints flag. Wall-clock timing itself is NOT expected
  to be bit-identical run to run (ordinary OS/CPU scheduling variance,
  unlike the onset-F1 scores above, which measure the separated AUDIO's
  content, not how long it took to produce) — the range above is reported
  honestly rather than a single misleadingly-precise number.
- Job progress: the `separate` stage is its own pipeline stage
  (`STAGE_PROGRESS["separate"] = 18`, between probe's 10 and normalize's
  25), so the Home screen's existing per-stage progress label shows
  "separate" while this runs, giving the user a real reason for the extra
  wait rather than a silent stall.

## Stem-mapping decision

This is the part of this item that most benefited from measuring instead
of assuming. The investigation went through two qualitative rounds during
initial development (both predate the determinism fix above, so their
EXACT F1 numbers are not independently reproducible the way the final
tables above are — the qualitative finding each round produced is what
carried forward into the shipped code, re-verified by the final,
deterministic numbers above):

**Round 1 (looked clean, was wrong): target the "other" stem alone.**
`htdemucs_6s` was tested on a synthetic guitar-melody-plus-vocal-and-
percussion mix: `other`-alone scored far better than the model's OWN
dedicated `guitar` stem on that same fixture, and better than the raw
unseparated mix. This looked like a clean, unambiguous win and was the
first thing committed to `aura_worker.separation`.

**Round 2 (a second, real fixture surfaced the flaw): a guitar-over-pad
mix.** Testing a SECOND mixed fixture -- a guitar arpeggio over a
sustained pad chord, no drums/vocals-like content at all -- with
`other`-alone separation produced **0 detected notes** (a
`NO_MUSIC_DETECTED` failure, not just a quality regression). The
clean-guitar-suite pass (separation ON, no interference at all) also
regressed multiple fixtures. Diagnosed directly (not guessed): a per-stem
energy probe on the pad fixture showed `other` stem RMS at the ~0.05 noise
floor shared by `drums`/`bass`/`vocals`/`piano` on that fixture, while the
model's own `guitar` stem carried RMS more than 3x higher -- **the model
had put almost the entire real guitar signal into its OWN `guitar` stem
this time**, the opposite of what happened with vocal+percussion
interference present. A fixed single-stem target is fundamentally the
wrong strategy for this model: which stem carries the real signal depends
on what else is in the mix. (This diagnostic used the pre-fix, `shifts=1`
code path too, but the qualitative pattern it found -- "which stem is
loud" is a stark, order-of-magnitude difference, not something a random
shift's few-percent perturbation could produce by chance.)

**Fix: sum the `guitar` and `other` stems.** This is the shipped mapping.
Re-verified with the determinism fix in place (the tables at the top of
this document): the guitar+vocal+percussion fixture's win is fully
preserved (F1 1.000, delta +0.200), the pad fixture is now a wash rather
than a failure (F1 0.762, delta +0.000 -- exactly the OFF baseline, not
degraded), and the clean-suite regressions are real but bounded (worst
case -0.222, 3 of 7 fixtures unchanged or improved) -- see
`aura_worker.separation`'s module docstring for the same evidence trail
inline with the code.

**Piano was never viable with this stem mapping.** A real-piano-sample
mixed fixture (piano chords + vocal + percussion, per this item's own
"real-piano samples layered with synthesized vocals" guidance) showed the
piano-instrument code path (dedicated `piano` stem, tested in isolation
during investigation, not the shipped `guitar`+`other` mapping which is
guitar-specific) landing at or below the unseparated baseline -- consistent
with `htdemucs_6s`'s own upstream documentation explicitly warning "the
piano source doesn't work so well at the moment". `aura_worker.separation`
and `aura_worker.stages.separate` are therefore guitar-only: a piano
project with the setting enabled is a documented no-op -- confirmed
directly in this report's own mixed-fixture row above and reproduced
bit-identically across all 4 runs (`piano_chords_mixed_vocal_percussion`'s
OFF and ON columns are IDENTICAL, 0.3125 both times, every run).

## Ship decision

**Shipped: opt-in, guitar only.** All hard constraints are met, with one
disclosed risk that has since been formally accepted:

- **Determinism**: fixed and independently re-verified 4x identical (see
  "Determinism" above) -- this was the one real defect a code review
  found in the first version of this item, and it is now closed with a
  regression test, not just a one-time fix.
- **Pins**: clean on Linux/Windows/macOS-arm64; one disclosed, narrow,
  non-build-verified caveat on macOS Intel (see "Resolution check" above).
- **Weights license**: MIT code; the pretrained weights carry a real,
  disclosed provenance risk (MUSDB18-HQ's non-commercial training-data
  license). **Accepted by the product owner on 2026-08-22**, for this
  app's personal-use context, after the disclosure above was surfaced --
  this closes the "a human reviewer should weigh in" item as a decision,
  not an open question.
- **CPU time**: ~1.5 minutes of extra compute for a 5-minute song
  (re-measured after the determinism fix -- actually faster than the
  pre-fix estimate, since removing the random-shift augmentation removed
  an extra forward pass), comfortably under the 10-minutes-extra
  threshold. A `separate` job stage makes the wait visible, not silent.
- **Benchmark**: a real, reproducible win on the interference pattern this
  item exists for (vocals + percussion, the shape of a real YouTube-import
  mix), no measurable cost on a harder interference pattern (a sustained
  pad, delta now +0.000 after the determinism fix), and a real, disclosed
  cost on clean solo audio (worst case -0.222, 3 of 7 fixtures unchanged
  or improved) -- an honest tradeoff of an OPT-IN feature a user chooses
  specifically because their recording has more than just the target
  instrument in it, not a feature that changes anything for anyone who
  leaves it unchecked. The Home screen's own inline copy tells the user
  this directly.

If a stricter reading of the weights-license risk is wanted despite the
acceptance above: the revert is small and localized -- the `separate`
stage's call site in `aura_worker.runner.run_transcription_job` and
`aura_worker.eval.pipeline.run_pipeline_stages` is a single `if` gate;
removing it (and leaving `aura_worker.separation`/`aura_worker.stages.
separate`/the fetch script/the frontend checkbox in place, inert) fully
disables the feature without touching anything else.
